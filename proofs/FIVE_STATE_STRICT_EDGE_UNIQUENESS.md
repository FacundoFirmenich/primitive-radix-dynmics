# Five-state strict-edge uniqueness

**Status:** PROVED_COMPUTER_ASSISTED_PLUS_SYMBOLIC  
**Date:** 2026-11-14

Every five-state nonpermutation finite high component has exactly one strict edge among its ten functional edges.

## Exact finite layer

The labelled functional jurisdiction contains 156,250 assignments in the two witness architectures admitted by the cycle budget. After strong-connectivity, crossing, diameter and cycle-budget screens, the possibility of two strict edges reduces to 840 canonical certificate profiles.

Their exact partition is

[
840=728+56+50+6.
]

- 728 profiles have rational Farkas certificates for an empty high cone;
- 56 force state collisions;
- 50 violate defect-one fibre injectivity;
- 6 reach the terminal arithmetic layer.

The canonical profiles and the exact exclusion tables are versioned under `research/five-state/certificates/`.

## Terminal symbolic layer

Four labelled factor variants have two strict T-edges into one target. After the fibre quotient they satisfy

[
BCequiv rAequiv sApmod Q,qquad r,sin{2,3}.
]

If `r=s`, the source states collide. If `r != s`, primitivity gives `Q | (r-s)` and hence `Q=1`, impossible.

The remaining factor variants have one strict T-edge and one strict S-edge at the same source. A defect-one fixed edge gives `B=1 (mod Q)`, while the strict edge gives `Bs=r (mod Q)`; again either the source collides or `Q | (r-s)`.

Thus no two-strict profile is realizable. The higher-strict cases are excluded by the finite compatibility gate, so exactly one strict edge remains.

## Boundary

This theorem is finite exact in functional-graph space. It is not a base scan or a denominator/height scan.
