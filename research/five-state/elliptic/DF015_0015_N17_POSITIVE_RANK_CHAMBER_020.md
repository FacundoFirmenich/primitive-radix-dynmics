# RGΦ Round 020-B — a positive-rank chamber component for \(n=17\)

Consider the Round-019 cubic \(F_{17}=0\). It contains the primitive integral point

\[
\boxed{P=[64233:11741:1138]}.
\]

## Exact checks

\[
F_{17}(64233,11741,1138)=0,
\]

and

\[
64233>5\cdot11741>1138>0.
\]

Moreover,

\[
64233\mid11741^3+1138^3,
\]

with quotient

\[
\frac{11741^3+1138^3}{64233}=25220421.
\]

Thus \(P\) satisfies the elliptic equation, the strict chamber inequalities and the cubic divisibility.

Set

\[
C=2A-z=116725,
\qquad
D=2A-w=127328.
\]

Then

\[
\gcd(C,D)=23.
\]

The two cycle quotients are

\[
\frac{(2z-3w)(2z-w)}D=\frac{80997}{23},
\]

\[
\frac{(7z+2w)(11z-2w)}C=\frac{2111575}{23}.
\]

Hence this point is a certified **near realization**: both failures have the same exact denominator \(23\), precisely the common factor excluded by the coprime-completion theorem.

Finally,

\[
\frac gC-\frac fD+5w+85z=17A.
\]

## Trivial rational torsion

Let

\[
O=[0:1:-1]
\]

be the rational origin used in Round 019. Exact projective enumeration gives good reductions

\[
\#F_{17}(\mathbb F_7)=10,
\quad
\#F_{17}(\mathbb F_{11})=14,
\quad
\#F_{17}(\mathbb F_{13})=18,
\quad
\#F_{17}(\mathbb F_{17})=23.
\]

The curve is smooth at each of these primes. For every possible prime divisor \(\ell\) of rational torsion, reduction at good primes different from \(\ell\) forces \(\ell\) to divide the corresponding finite group orders. The displayed orders have no common prime divisor compatible with this requirement. Therefore

\[
\boxed{F_{17}(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}=0}.
\]

Since \(P\ne O\), the point \(P\) has infinite order. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}F_{17}(\mathbb Q)\ge1}.
\]

## Infinitely many rational chamber points

The real points of a smooth projective cubic form a compact one-dimensional Lie group. The closure of the subgroup generated by a non-torsion point contains a real circle component. The inequalities

\[
A>5z>w>0
\]

define an open neighbourhood of \(P\) on the real curve. Therefore infinitely many rational multiples of \(P\) return to this neighbourhood.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
F_{17}=0\text{ contains infinitely many rational points in the chamber }A>5z>w>0.
}
\]

Real/chamber geometry cannot eliminate `DF015-0015`. Any global closure must use the arithmetic filters — especially coprimality and the cubic divisibility — not only the positivity inequalities.

This theorem does **not** produce a valid RGΦ realization: the displayed point fails the two cycle divisibilities by the common factor \(23\).
